> **Read this first.**
> - **Not legal advice.** Every source below must still go through the license, consent, privacy,
>   and takedown review that blueprint §8 requires. A permissive tag on a dataset card usually
>   covers the *annotations or URL list only*, not the underlying images, audio, video, or text.

>   blocks huggingface.co, arxiv.org, and most project sites, so Hugging Face card and paper facts

## 1. Key findings

1. **Text and code are not the constraint; licensing nuance is.**
   - Over 10T tokens of filtered, ODC-By/CC-BY web text exist: FineWeb-Edu, FineWeb2, FinePDFs,
     DCLM-Baseline, and the Dolma 3 pool.
   - Openly licensed corpora exist at useful scale: Common Corpus ~2T tokens and Common Pile ~8 TB.
   - The work is excluding sources with research-only clauses, bespoke agreements, or pirated books.

2. **Media is data-limited, not compute-limited.** At the blueprint's default encoding rates the
   curriculum needs:
   - ~18.7M hours of unique video;
   - ~21.8M hours of audio;
   - ~9.8M hours of synchronized audio-video.

   What actually exists is far smaller:
   - The largest hosted, nearly cleared video set found is FineVideo, ~3,425 h [✓].
   - Reasonably cleared unique speech is ~2.5–3M h after deduplication [S].

   Even with heavy repetition and richer per-second encodings, the video and audio-video shares
   need **licensed or commissioned media, or a smaller share** (§2).

3. **Almost every large video-caption corpus is non-commercial, YouTube-ID-only, or both** [GH].
   This covers Panda-70M, HD-VILA-100M, InternVid, Koala-36M, HowTo100M, YT-Temporal-1B, and
   Action100M. WebVid was withdrawn after a Shutterstock cease-and-desist [GH].

4. **No public dataset combines commercially usable rights, original synchronized audio, and
   edit-level labels** (cuts, transitions, caption timing). Edit data has to be built: licensed
   footage, plus synthetic timeline edits with deterministic verifiers, plus commissioned editor
   sessions (§5, §8).

5. **Many open instruction sets are distilled from proprietary models**, and those models' terms
   apply. Examples: GPT-4/4o/4V in Cambrian, LLaVA-Video-178K, and ShareGPT4Video; Gemini in
   PixelProse and FineVideo annotations; Claude in CoSyn and PixMo-Docs.
   - Prefer human-annotated data, programmatic data, or data generated by open-weight models whose
     terms allow it (DeepSeek-R1 is MIT and explicitly allows distillation [S]).
   - Watch for flow-down clauses (Llama 3.1's naming requirement [S]).

6. **Some well-known benchmarks are no longer clean holdouts.**
   - MMMU test answers were released on 2026-02-12 [✓].
   - MLVU test ground truth was released on 2025-03-18 [S].
   - Competition math (AIME/AMC) sits inside NuminaMath, Big-Math, and DeepScaleR [S].

   Use benchmarks with private test labels (MathVista test, MMAU test, LongVideoBench test) and
   in-house editing holdouts split by source event (§9).

7. **Rights risk is concentrated and avoidable.**
   - Pirated books: Books3 was taken down; the Bartz v. Anthropic $1.5B settlement received final
     approval in July 2026 [✓].
   - CSAM: found in LAION-5B, which was re-released as Re-LAION.
   - YouTube-derived media.
   - Voice and face biometrics.
   - Non-commercial labels hidden behind permissive tags.

   §10 lists each.

## 2. What the curriculum actually needs

Blueprint §8 sets **4.75T positions** across Stages A–D. Applying each stage's sampling shares
gives this budget (trillions of positions):

| Stage | Total | Text/code | Image | Video | Audio | Sync AV |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A: joint foundation | 3.50 | 2.100 | 0.350 | 0.350 | 0.350 | 0.350 |
| B: capability mid-training | 0.75 | 0.375 | 0.090 | 0.105 | 0.090 | 0.090 |
| C: long context | 0.35 | 0.193 | 0.035 | 0.053 | 0.035 | 0.035 |
| D: memory, ultra-long | 0.15 | 0.068 | 0.008 | 0.030 | 0.015 | 0.030 |
| **Total** | **4.75** | **2.735** | **0.483** | **0.538** | **0.490** | **0.505** |

**Code default since the design review.** `data.mixture.STAGES` now makes Stage A text-heavy
(`docs/IMPROVEMENTS.md` §4): a 0.35T-position text-only warm-up, then 70 / 12 / 5 / 8 / 5. Stage A
becomes 2.555 / 0.378 / 0.158 / 0.252 / 0.158T, and the totals become **3.190 / 0.511 / 0.345 / 0.392 /
0.313T**: about 36% fewer video positions, 20% fewer audio, and 38% fewer synchronized AV. The hours
below use the blueprint table (`data.mixture.BLUEPRINT_STAGES`), so they are an upper bound.

**Converting positions to raw data.** Media-only rates from blueprint §5: video 8 positions/s
(0.5 fps × 16 tokens) by default and 64/s at high detail; audio 6.25/s, or 12.5/s at high fidelity.
Paired caption or transcript text adds a few percent and slightly lowers the hours needed.

| Modality | Unique hours needed with no repetition | With ≤4 epochs | What exists, rights-cleared or nearly so |
|---|---|---|---|
| Video | 18.7M h at 8 pos/s; 4.7M h at 32/s; 2.3M h at 64/s | 4.7M / 1.17M / 0.58M h | FineVideo 3,425 h [✓]. Per-item-licensed DiDeMo and stock or public-domain collections are small [U]. **Licensed footage must fill the rest.** |
| Audio | 21.8M h at 6.25/s; 10.9M h at 12.5/s | 5.4M / 2.7M h | ~2.5–3M unique h of speech after deduplication [S]. General audio is in the thousands of hours (AudioSet ~5.8k h, YouTube-sourced) [S]. |
| Sync AV | 9.8M h at 14.25/s; 3.7M h at 38.25/s | 2.5M / 0.92M h | Essentially none with commercial rights and edit labels. |
| Image | 3.4–7.5B examples at 144–64 positions each | ~1–2B unique, 2–4 epochs | DataComp/CommonPool 12.8B URL pairs [S]; COYO 747M [GH]; MINT-1T 3.4B images [S]; OBELICS 353M [S]; PD12M 12.4M [S]. **Sufficient after re-filtering.** The license covers the URL tables, not the images. |
| Text/code | 2.7T positions | 1–2 epochs on top tiers | Well over 10T filtered tokens [S]. **Sufficient.** |

**Recommendations.**

1. Treat the Stage A video, audio, and AV percentages as *upper bounds*. Set them from the
   compute-normalised pilot measurements §8 already requires, and from what has actually been
   licensed.
2. Prefer **richer encodings of fewer, better hours**:
   - high-detail video (1 fps × 64 tokens) on licensed footage;
   - 12.5 tokens/s audio on high-quality speech;
   - default rates only for abundant data.

   This cuts the unique hours needed by up to 8×.
3. Cap epochs per quality tier:

   | Tier | Epoch cap |
   |---|---|
   | Human-transcribed speech | ~8 |
   | Cleaned pseudo-labelled speech | 3–5 |
   | Raw speech with teacher labels | 1–2 |
   | Small human-annotated vision sets | ~10 |
   | Rare general-audio sets | 20–30, with augmentation |

   These caps are the surveys' recommendations, not published laws.
4. Set a **media procurement target** before Stage A. Example: ≤4 epochs at 32 video positions/s
   needs **~1.2M licensed video hours**, of which ~0.9M should keep their original soundtrack for
   the AV share.
   - If procurement falls short, return the remainder to text/code and images, which are not
     data-limited. Do not over-repeat noisy media.

| ~4% | Long documents: FinePDFs English | ~0.1T |

By stage:
- **Stage B** follows the OLMo 3 Dolmino / SmolLM3 decay-stage pattern: high-score web, FineMath-4+,
  Stack-Edu, peS2o, FinePDFs-Edu, Wikipedia.
- **Stage C** uses repo-level permissive code, long PDFs, full papers, and public-domain books,
  mixed with short high-quality data. The ProLong paper found this mixing necessary.
- **Stage D** uses the longest licensed documents plus synthetic long-context tasks built only
  from licensed text.

Deduplicate across all pools before counting tokens: Zyda-2 and Dolma 3 overlap DCLM and FineWeb-Edu.

**The central gap.** No verified public dataset combines all three of:
- commercially usable rights;
- the original synchronized soundtrack;
- edit-level labels (cut type, transition, pacing, caption timing, B-roll choice).

Every edit-grammar set found (AVE, MovieCuts, Condensed Movies, ShotBench, Shot2Story) is film
clips or non-commercial [GH].

**How to close it.**

1. **License media.** Stock and creator footage under AI-training licenses, broadcaster archives,
   and public-domain collections. Target the hours in §2. Require original audio, shot metadata,
   and model releases for identifiable people.
2. **Commission annotation.** Editors cut licensed raw footage in a real NLE and export
   OTIO / EDL / FCPXML. Record their tool-call traces with consent; these become orchestrator
   trajectories (§8).
3. **Synthetic edits with verifiers.**
   - Generate programmatic OTIO edits (trims, transitions, captions, L/J cuts) on licensed footage
     and render them with ffmpeg.
   - Keep each `(timeline_before, operations, timeline_after, render)` tuple.
   - Verify every tuple deterministically:
     - a shot-boundary detector confirms each cut within ±1 frame;
     - ASR/OCR checks caption timing;
     - audio alignment checks L/J cuts;
     - the repository's own `agent/timeline.py` probes run on the result.

   This also supplies Stage D long-horizon tasks, following LongVALE's timestamped AV-event format.
4. **Recaption in-house.** Use an open-weight captioner trained on cleared data, label the captions
   synthetic, and reject any caption that fails temporal-grounding checks (§8 of the blueprint).

**No public (instruction, timeline-before, operations, timeline-after) dataset was found** [GH].
The closest is ExpressEdit: 50 intents, no state, no license. Because huggingface.co and arxiv.org
were unreachable, read this as "not found", not "does not exist".

**Orchestrator data plan.** This is what this repository is built to generate.

1. **Canonical state.** Use OTIO JSON as the timeline of record, with Apache-2.0 adapters for
   FCPXML, EDL, and AAF. `agent/timeline.py` already provides the deterministic `apply_edit@2`
   semantics and the timeline, render, and transition probes. Compile to MLT or ffmpeg for real
   render probes.
2. **Generator.** Sample timelines and operation programs from the versioned `ToolRegistry`
   (trim, split, ripple-delete, insert, transition, retime, caption, duck, grade), then apply them
   deterministically to get the after-state. Write instructions from templates and paraphrase them
   with a self-hosted, permissively licensed model.
3. **Content-grounded tasks** ("cut where she says X"). Use owned or licensed media plus TTS so the
   ground truth is known.
4. **Verifiers double as RL terminal rewards.** These are the §9 verifiable rewards:
   - schema-valid envelope;
   - argument unit tests;
   - canonical OTIO diff equals the target within a frame tolerance;
   - render probes: duration, sampled frame hashes, loudness and silence at cuts, OCR of burned-in
     captions;
   - idempotent replay;
   - receipt signatures.

   The `ModelPlanner` + `ControlPlane` + `Orchestrator` loop already emits these as receipts and
   trajectories.
5. **Failure and recovery.** Inject faults:
   - timeouts and version skew;
   - stale state and missing media;
   - partial renders;
   - permission denials and concurrent edits.

   Keep success, failure, and recovery traces; recovered-vs-failed pairs become preference data.
   Add SWE-smith-style "break a valid timeline, repair it" tasks.
6. **Escalation labels.** Run the small model k times per task and label "escalate" where its
   *verified* pass rate falls below a threshold. Add it as a fifth class next to When2Call's four.
   This feeds `agent/escalation.py`'s logistic router.
7. **Safety episodes.**
   - Injections in clip metadata, burned-in frames (FigStep-style), TTS audio, tool outputs, and
     memory, following BIPIA, InjecAgent, AgentDojo, and the NeMo Gym environment.
   - Cross-session poisoned memory (MINJA / AgentPoison methods).
   - Confirmation-gated destructive operations.

   `eval/agent_security.py` already scores the control-plane side of these.
8. **Harness.** NeMo Gym or OpenEnv, with tau2's LLM user simulator for multi-turn revision sessions.
9. **Commissioned traces.** Real editors working on licensed footage (§5), recorded with consent,
   anchor the synthetic distribution.

## 10. Rights red flags, acquisition plan, governance

**Red flags, consolidated.**

1. **Pirated books.**
   - Books3: DMCA takedown in August 2023 [S].
   - RedPajama-1T's book subset is defunct, and SlimPajama still lists `the_pile_books3` [S].
   - LibGen / PiLiMi: the Bartz v. Anthropic $1.5B settlement received final approval on
     2026-07-20 [✓] ([Authors Alliance](https://www.authorsalliance.org/2026/07/21/bartz-v-anthropic-settlement-receives-final-approval/)).
   - Exclude all of these, including ProLong's books subset.
2. **CSAM.** LAION-5B had 2,236 links removed for Re-LAION [S]. Every image and web pipeline needs
   its own CSAM-hash and NSFW filtering, whatever upstream did.
3. **Takedowns and withdrawals.**
   - WebVid, after a Shutterstock cease-and-desist [GH].
   - Deleted source videos (~3% of DiDeMo, >25% of YouCook2) must be treated as withdrawn [GH][S].
4. **Non-commercial or academic-only terms, often behind a permissive tag:**
   - Panda-70M, HD-VILA, Koala, InternVid, Action100M;
   - LLaVA-OneVision-Data, LLaVA-Video-178K, Honey-Data;
   - Institutional Books [✓];
   - Emilia, GigaSpeech 2, WavCaps, AudioCaps, MTG-Jamendo, KodCode, RLAIF-V, VLFeedback, Nectar;
   - Audio Flamingo data.
5. **Proprietary-model output terms.**
   - OpenAI-generated: Cambrian gpt4v/gpt4o subsets, LLaVA-Video, ShareGPT4Video, olmOCR-mix,
     OpenHermes, UltraFeedback ratings.
   - Google: PixelProse and FineVideo annotations (Gemini).
   - Anthropic: CoSyn, PixMo-Docs.
   - Open-model flow-down: Llama 3.1 naming clause (Magpie, Llama-Nemotron), Qwen and DeepSeek
     notices on Nemotron v2.
6. **URL-only licenses.** CommonPool, COYO, OBELICS, VGGSound, and AudioSet license the index, not
   the media. OBELICS also left opted-out documents in place [S].
7. **YouTube-derived media.** YODAS (CC BY flag set by the uploader), Granary, Emilia, GigaSpeech,
   AudioSet, VGGSound, FineVideo, and all ID-only video corpora. YouTube's terms on downloading were
   not checked [U].
8. **Biometrics and privacy.**
   - Voice is personal data under GDPR and a "voiceprint" under Illinois BIPA [U: statutes not fetched].
   - Faces in AVSpeech, VoxCeleb, LRS, and Ego4D bystanders.
   - Speaker-ID or diarization training on web voices needs legal review.
9. **Common Crawl.** Publishers object to paywalled content and report unfulfilled removals;
   Common Crawl disputes this [S]. Add a publisher-domain blocklist and an opt-out filter to every
   CC-derived corpus.
10. **Share-alike and copyleft.** Wikipedia, Infinity-Instruct, People's Speech subsets, CHiME-6,
    and the AudioSet ontology are share-alike; ChartQA is GPL-3.0.
11. **Tool-use and agent data.**
    - APIGen-MT and ActionStudio are CC-BY-NC.
    - ToolBench is OpenAI-generated and research-only.
    - Gated click-through terms: xlam-60k.
    - Tensor Trust and ExpressEdit have no license file.
    - NeMo Gym licenses environments individually; some are internal-only, GPL, or CC-BY-SA.
    - GPL and LGPL render tools (MLT, Kdenlive, ffmpeg with `--enable-gpl`) are fine as sandboxed
      subprocesses, but redistributing linked binaries carries obligations [GH].
12. **Conflicting license metadata**, to be resolved before ingesting:
    - Granary (CC-BY 3.0 vs 4.0);
    - Libriheavy (Apache vs CC BY);
    - DiPCo (CDLA 1.0 vs 2.0);
    - OmniCorpus (CC BY vs Apache);
    - DenseFusion (CC-BY vs MIT);
    - MiraData (contradictory clauses);
    - MUSIC-AVQA (GPL vs MIT).

**Acquisition plan.** In order of impact on the §2 gap:

1. **License media.** Target ~1.2M video hours with original sound, and premium speech in
   under-covered languages. Contracts should cover AI training, derivatives, takedown handling, and
   model releases.
2. **Commission annotation.**
   - Editor sessions on licensed footage, exported as OTIO, with consented tool traces.
   - Human grounded captions on a stratified sample, used to calibrate synthetic captioners.
   - Human preference judgements on editing outcomes.
3. **Synthesise with verifiers.**
   - Timeline edits and renders (§5).
   - SynthDoG-style documents with licensed backgrounds.
   - Chart and table renders.
   - Long-context tasks over licensed text.
   - Use only teacher models whose terms allow it, and label everything synthetic.
4. **Consented user data**, opt-in only, later, with deletion propagated through manifests.

**Governance, mapped to this repository.**

- Register every source as a `SourceRecord` in `m63_omni/data/manifest.py`, with `license`,
  `rights_status`, `consent`, `takedown`, `modality`, and `synthetic`.
- `DataManifest.build` already **refuses** a source unless its rights are cleared, it has no
  takedown, and consent is in place. Every entry in this survey therefore starts as
  `rights_status: pending`.
- The ten-stage pipeline (`m63_omni/data/pipeline.py`) runs these checks on every source:
  - deduplication;
  - PII and safety filtering;
  - decontamination against §9;
  - media integrity and alignment;
  - manifest generation.
- Takedowns trigger manifest regeneration, and retraining or patching as blueprint §15 requires.
